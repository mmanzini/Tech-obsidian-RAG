# Max Schoening — Agency, Malleable Software, and the Tiny Core

**Source:** [Lenny's Podcast — "AI era skills: Why cultivating agency matters more than job titles"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-cultivating-agency-matters-more)
**Author:** Max Schoening (Head of Product, Notion; formerly GitHub, Heroku, Google PM, 2× founder)
**Channel:** Lenny's Podcast

---

## Summary

Max Schoening argues that agency — not skills — is the thing that separates people who thrive in the AI era from those who fall behind. Skills are now available at your fingertips via AI; what's scarce is the drive to reach for them. He introduces the "tiny core" theory of product excellence, the concept of malleable software, and the "first 10% free" principle, while arguing that the SaaS apocalypse is greatly exaggerated and that taste can be deliberately trained.

## Agency as the Core Differentiator

Before AI, it was easy to say "I'll never do this because of a skill gap." Now models close those gaps — which means agency is the actual scarce resource (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md). Agency is the understanding that the world around you is malleable and was built by people no smarter than you. Schoening's advice: "Drive Notion like it's stolen" — act on the company as if you have full licence to change things, not just your assigned domain. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

Agency is developed through making. Tinkering builds the mental model that things can be changed, which awakens you to the deeper truth: you have far more capacity to shape the world than you thought. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

**Notion examples:** Brian Lovin (designer+engineer who also became Notion's number-one internal recruiter, well outside his formal role); Eric Lou (PM who shifted from PRDs → Figma → prototypes by deciding to build the skills that would make him hirable as a founding-team member). (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

## The First 10% Free Principle

The first 10% of every project is now effectively free — it takes almost no effort to build the first version of a startup. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md) The implication: the cost of exploration has collapsed. You can afford to send ten agents down ten paths and see which one is right. "Demos not memos" and "give me something to react to" become structurally easier. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

But the last 10% is still actually 90% of the work. Feature count, tokens consumed, and lines of code are all the same silly metric — what matters is: does the product have an obviously good tiny core? (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

## The Tiny Core Theory

Every truly great product has one tiny thing that is a superpower: (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

- **iPhone** — multitouch
- **GitHub** — the pull request (anyone can suggest a change and you see it)
- **Notion** — blocks + slash commands
- **Dropbox** — the menu bar sync icon (so reliable it doubled as an internet-connection indicator)
- **Heroku** — `git push heroku master` (zero friction from code to URL)

The biggest product pitfall: "if I add one more feature it'll finally be great." That never works. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

Being first doesn't matter — being right matters. Anthropic started after OpenAI, got less funding, and ended up winning by being right twice (Dario Hassabis doing the same thing successfully at two different organisations). (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

## Malleable Software

Malleable software works closer to the interest of the people who use it than the corporation that makes it. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md) The current world of apps is a fixed landscape — you cannot rearrange your house because someone else decided the kitchen layout. Schoening wants software that lets users own their computing life, adapt tools to their needs, and build on top of common platforms without forking everything into isolated personal tools.

Notion is beginning to approach this — it looks more like an operating system than a traditional SaaS, with malleable blocks and a slash-command layer that can be extended. AI agents roaming a connected workspace make the value of malleability concrete for the first time: agents need loose, traversable structure, not narrow API orifices. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

The Dieter Rams reference: design should be first useful, then beautiful. The canonical products in a museum — including Gehry-designed chairs — that look spectacular but function poorly embody the wrong priority. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

## SaaS Apocalypse is Exaggerated

The "as-a-service" part is the thing that actually matters — people don't want to maintain the full stack. SaaS in the 2010s was often just a fancy form around a spreadsheet; that vertical is at risk. But the specialist thinking, the maintenance, the years of user-signal accumulation — those aren't going away. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

Software as a garden: you need to tend it. The Slack notification-decision flowchart (the internal routing logic for whether and how to show a notification) represents decades of user-signal. Nobody vibe-coding a Slack replacement will replicate that. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

## Coding as Understanding the Material

Schoening doesn't care whether designers or PMs ship code to production. What he cares about is whether they *understand the medium*. The only way to understand agent loops is to build them in code. Designers who prototype in code are designing in the actual final material, not a static approximation. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

Physical metaphor: prototypes made with 3D printers show layer lines — obviously not the final product. The engineering equivalent is the yield-optimised factory. Most AI discourse skips this: it's all about shipping features, not about making the factory work for a billion people. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

Designers and PMs at Notion are increasingly in the terminal, using Claude Code and Codex. The CLI initially frightens them; once they're in it, curiosity pulls them deeper. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

## Building Taste

Taste = the ability to run a virtual machine in your head where, given an idea, you can predict whether a chosen in-group will like it. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md) It is trained the same way models are trained: reps with feedback. High-taste designers have two things in common: side projects where they own the whole thing end-to-end, and constant exposure to new apps (they're the "annoying person" who suggests 49 new tools). (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

Notion conference rooms are named after iconic objects (first typewriter, Macintosh, Porsche 911) — sitting in them with attention reminds you that nothing you're currently building meets that bar. (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md)

## Roles Merging — What We Might Lose

The specialist risk: as roles merge (engineer/PM/designer), we risk losing deep specialists on the edges. The mass-generalisation discourse focuses on "how many tokens/features can we ship" but misses the factory-engineering question — how do you make the thing work for 100 million people at high precision? (source: transcript-ai-era-skills-max-schoening-notion.md) Design delight and craft are also at risk of collapsing into generic outputs from the same design systems.

## Key Takeaways

- Agency (not skills) is the scarce resource in the AI era; skills are at your fingertips via AI.
- Every great product has a tiny core that is a superpower — stop adding features before finding it.
- The first 10% of any project is now free; the last 10% is still 90% of the work.
- Malleable software = software that serves the user's interest over the corporation's; Notion-as-OS is an early example.
- SaaS-as-a-service isn't dying — the specialist maintenance and accumulated user-signal are the moat, not the form-wrapper.
- Taste is trainable: reps with feedback, side projects end-to-end, constant exposure to other people's work.
